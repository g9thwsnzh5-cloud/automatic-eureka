## 1. Did a significant rotation out of AI/tech/semis happen, and what triggered it?

### Takeaway
Yes. Price data shows a sharp rotation, and it peaked in October 2026. The Nasdaq Composite fell about 5.3% and the Nasdaq-100 fell about 6.7% in October, the worst month for both since March 2025. Over the same month the Dow rose 1.7%, 9 of the 11 S&P sectors gained, and the equal-weight S&P 500 beat the cap-weighted index by 5.7 percentage points, its biggest monthly margin since April 2009. The rotation continued on Nov 2. Several triggers piled up:
- OpenAI's revenue disclosure on Oct 8
- worries about how AI is being financed, plus rising global rates
- hyperscaler earnings on Oct 28–29: Alphabet had its worst day on record and capex stayed high
- Oracle's and CoreWeave's debt and equity raises

### Inferences
- Price evidence of rotation is strong and has several confirming signals:
  - the equal-weight S&P beat the cap-weighted index by 5.7pp, a 17-year extreme
  - 9 of 11 sectors gained
  - the Dow rose 1.7% while the Nasdaq fell about 5.3%
- The "rapid" part fits a window of roughly Oct 6 to Nov 2. The late-October leg (Oct 28 to Nov 2) was driven by Alphabet's earnings reaction, Intel's crash, and Oracle's and CoreWeave's financing news.
- The move is a rotation rather than a market crash. The S&P 500 fell only 0.9% in October, and on Nov 2 the S&P and Nasdaq were still up year-to-date ([STL.News](https://www.stl.news/stock-market-today-monday-november-2-2026/)).
- A rough estimate, not reported by any source: if the cap-weighted S&P 500 fell 0.9% and equal-weight beat it by 5.7pp, the equal-weight S&P 500 gained about 4.8% in October.

## 2. Fund flows: did money actually leave AI/tech funds?

### Takeaway
The flow evidence is mixed, and that matters.

**Active and hedge-fund money sold tech heavily in mid-October:**
- Goldman's prime-brokerage data shows hedge funds selling tech at the fastest pace in at least a decade in the five sessions to Oct 15.
- BofA clients sold a record $14.8B of single stocks, including $9.1B of tech, in a week around Oct 13–17.

**Fund-level flows for the latest week did not show an exit from tech:**
- LSEG Lipper shows US tech sector funds took in +$4.54B in the week to Oct 28, even as US equity funds overall lost $6.65B.
- An EPFR-based report shows +$5.5B into global tech funds in the same week.

So the rotation is clearer in prices and in institutional or hedge-fund positioning than in ETF and mutual-fund flows.

### Inferences
- The "rotation" is mainly a story of prices and of hedge-fund and institutional positioning, through de-grossing and tax-loss selling. Retail and passive investors kept buying tech: Lipper +$4.54B and EPFR +$5.5B in the week to Oct 28, plus $2.6B of retail semiconductor buying in mid-October.
- That split between flows and prices supports a reading of positioning and valuation de-risking rather than a capitulation-style exodus.
- Possible risk: if retail inflows reverse, selling pressure could get worse.

## 3. How did Oracle (ORCL) trade, and how does it compare with peers?

### Takeaway
ORCL was one of the worst performers in the late-October leg of the selloff:
- Oct 28 (investor day): up about 11%, to roughly $144.54.
- Then it gave back all of that gain and more. On Nov 2, 2026 it closed at about $119.22 (-8.76%), its worst day since June and its lowest close since Jul 29.
- The three-session fall of about 18% was its worst three-day stretch since 2002.
- The trigger was Oracle raising, in one go, the entire $20B of new funding it had said it would raise through fiscal 2030: $10B of bonds plus $10B of stock, the stock priced at about $116.
- ORCL is about 33% lower year-to-date and about 65% below its record intraday high of $345.69 (Sep 10, 2025).

ORCL moved together with neocloud peers (CoreWeave, Nebius) on worries about AI debt. Microsoft, Meta and the broad indices held up much better.

## 4. Strategist views: temporary rotation or regime change?

### Takeaway
Most sell-side and institutional commentary calls this a rotation and repricing within an intact bull market: Goldman, JPMorgan Private Bank, Saxo, BlackRock and Wedbush. More cautious voices include Piper Sandler and BofA's Hartnett. Piper Sandler says the best of the AI theme may be over. Hartnett stays tactically risk-off until about mid-November or until the rate-hiking cycle ends, but prefers tech bonds over tech stocks.

### Inferences
- The consensus "rotation, not reckoning" view relies on three things holding: breadth (equal-weight and the Dow), continuing retail and passive inflows into tech, and no recession signal.
- A regime change would look like:
  - the Russell 2000 or equal-weight index starting to fall along with the Nasdaq
  - tech-fund flows turning negative
  - more financing stress among leveraged AI-infrastructure names (ORCL, CRWV) spreading to the hyperscalers' credit
- The direction of change in the shift from buybacks to issuance (Alphabet and Meta not buying back stock; Oracle's equity raise) is structurally negative for AI equity holders, and supports the cautious camp.
